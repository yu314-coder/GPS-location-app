# Recording a route without satellites

LaTeX source and PDF for the paper describing **Velocity Mode**: vibration-derived speed
estimation and GPS-free direction, their measured accuracy across the instrumented journeys, and the
approaches that were implemented, measured and rejected along the way.

## Build

    pdflatex velocity_mode.tex && pdflatex velocity_mode.tex

Twice, so the figure and table references resolve. Uses only standard packages (`lmodern`,
`amsmath`, `booktabs`, `longtable`, `tikz`, `natbib`, `fancyhdr`, `hyperref`, `float`), so it builds with
pdfLaTeX anywhere, arXiv included.

## Contents

| File | |
|---|---|
| `velocity_mode.tex` | Source |
| `velocity_mode.pdf` | Compiled, 29 pages: introduction, related work, method (with the recordings and how they are measured), results for each kind of travel in its own section — motorcycle, walking, car, plane — how the two built-in networks work, every kind of travel side by side, discussion, limitations, and appendices listing every journey and every setting |
| `figures/` | One figure per kind of travel (`fig_motorcycle`, `fig_walking`, `fig_car`, and for the plane `fig9_flightprofile` for the speed, `fig5_flight` for the direction, `fig10_flightroute`), the built-in network against a full store (`fig6_network`), and every kind of travel side by side (`fig_all`). The system diagram is drawn in the source, and the diagram of the two networks in `tables/fig_networks.tex` |
| `tables/` | Generated by the evaluation scripts from the re-run of the newest version: the recordings, each vehicle at a glance with its speed bands, walking, the flight, the starting network, every kind of travel side by side, what each part of the direction method adds, and every journey. Written by hand: approaches that did not work, settings, and the network diagram. `numbers.tex` holds every data-dependent number the text quotes, and `numbers.json` feeds the app's page |

Every number in the paper compares the latest version with GPS recorded at the same time and used
only as the answer key. Every recording was re-run through the latest app logic from its saved raw
sensor readings (acceleration, rotation and orientation angles at 50 Hz):
- speed: the model rebuilt as the app builds it, with a memory drawn from every other journey, plus
  its rules for stops, a handled phone and holding the last answer;
- walking: the step detector and its bookkeeping;
- direction and routes: the direction method, with the heading drift measured at stops taken off and,
  where the magnetic field was recorded, the clean-field magnetometer correction;
- the flights: the app's own Swift code for the takeoff integration and test, the typical flight and the
  direction in the air, compiled and run on the raw samples and the cabin's recorded pressure, against
  the short flight's GPS and the four-hour flight's public ADS-B track. The typical flight is the median of
  NASA's public DASHlink flight recorder data (302 flights of one regional jet) pooled with a day of the
  OpenSky Network's ADS-B state vectors (1,194 jet takeoffs), and is measured on those flights with each
  aircraft held out. OpenSky data: Schäfer et al., "Bringing up OpenSky: A large-scale ADS-B sensor network
  for research", IPSN 2014 (used for non-profit research). The plane's figures, its table and
  `tables/numbers_flight.tex` are written by `paper7_flight.py` and `paper7_tables.py`.
- the PDF carries a revision number in its keywords (`velocity-mode-paper revision N`, N = the app build it
  was made for), which the app compares to show the newest copy it has; it carries no dates.

The speed store for each recording is 4,000 examples drawn from every other recording. Each example
keeps a fixed chance of being drawn, so adding a recording changes the others only by its own examples.

`app_numbers.py` writes the numbers the app's "How Velocity Mode works" page shows from
`tables/numbers.json`, which the table generator writes with the tables, so the page and the paper
cannot drift apart.

Three inputs come from the recordings as they were: walking or riding, Apple's motion classifier,
and the car-park ramp flag. The phone's step counter and cabin pressure were not recorded. The paper
and its figures carry no recording dates or times.
